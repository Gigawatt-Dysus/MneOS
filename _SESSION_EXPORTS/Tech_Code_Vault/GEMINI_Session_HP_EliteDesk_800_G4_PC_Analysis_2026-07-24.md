**Gemini:** Alright Eric. Rescuing that hardware before the corporate overlords at Optum chucked it is a smart move, but your paranoia is absolutely justified. Enterprise machines are riddled with hardware-level management tools like Intel AMT and Windows Autopilot that will absolutely try to phone home the second they hit the internet.Here is exactly how you burn this EliteDesk down to the studs and sever all ties to the mothership.1. Nuke the BIOS and Disable Hardware ManagementThe first layer of corporate control lives in the UEFI. You need to turn off all the HP and Intel management modules.Boot the machine and smash F10 to get into the BIOS.Go to the Security tab.Find HP Sure Start and completely disable it. If left on, it can log events and mess with your boot process.Look for Intel Active Management Technology (AMT) or Intel ME. Uncheck it or set it to disabled.While you are in there, find the option to Unconfigure AMT on next boot and apply it to flush out any lingering network configurations.Check for anything labeled Absolute Persistence or Computrace and disable it. This is literal corporate spyware built into the motherboard.Save changes and reboot.2. Wipe the Drive BareDon't trust a simple quick format during the Windows setup. We want the drive wiped clean.Boot from your Windows installation USB.When you hit the first setup screen, press Shift + F10 to open the command prompt.Run diskpart.Run list disk to find your NVMe drive.Run select disk X (where X is your drive number).Run clean (or clean all if you want to zero the entire drive, which takes longer but is definitively thorough).Run exit and close the prompt.3. Evade Windows AutopilotIf the company registered this machine's hardware hash with Microsoft Endpoint Manager (Intune), the moment Windows 11 connects to Wi-Fi or Ethernet during setup, it will lock you into a corporate login screen.Crucial Step: Do not plug in an Ethernet cable, and do not connect to Wi-Fi during the Windows setup.When the setup gets to the "Let's connect you to a network" screen, it will likely hide the "I don't have internet" button.Press Shift + F10 again.Type OOBE\BYPASSNRO and hit Enter. The system will reboot.When you get back to the network screen, select "I don't have internet" and proceed with a limited setup to create a standard local admin account.Once you are on the desktop with your local account, it's safe to plug it into your network. Autopilot only triggers during the Out-Of-Box Experience setup phase.
