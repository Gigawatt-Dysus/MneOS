# Document: MneOS Nomenclature & "Own Self" Philosophy
## Sub-system: Core Identity / Architecture Manifest

**Author/Architect:** Eric Cornett

**Directive:** Ingest into system logs / architectural history base.

**Subject:** Architectural Re-branding – MneOS and the "Own Self" Directive

We are transitioning the project namespace from LifeOS to MneOS.

Within this architecture, the acronym OS no longer refers to a traditional "Operating System." It now explicitly stands for **Own Self**. This constant is both an imperative and a question, spawning directly from Polonius’s famous line in Shakespeare’s *Hamlet* (Act 1, Scene 3): *"To thine Own Self be true."*

The significance of this framework operates on two distinct layers—the modern interpretation and the original ironic context—both of which serve as system invariants for the application's behavior and user boundaries.

### 1. The Modern Meaning: Authenticity, Integrity, and Sovereignty
In modern application, this phrase acts as an inspiring anthem for self-discovery and personal truth. For MneOS, it serves as an architectural directive with three core pillars:

* **Radical Honesty:** The system functions as a secure repository to process lived experience and personal truth, ensuring the user does not live a lie or pretend to be someone they are not.
* **Sovereignty and Boundaries:** It is an imperative to maintain firm boundaries against adversarial, toxic, or transactional external forces—whether rigid bureaucracies, rigid institutions, or fair-weather acquaintances. It directs the user not to alter their character or yield to external pressure simply to get along.
* **Prioritizing Personal Well-being:** Sovereign care of one's own needs and moral compass comes first, not out of selfishness, but to ensure long-term stability and trustworthiness to everyone else.

### 2. The Original Meaning: The Irony of Polonius
In the original play, the phrase carries a more cynical, practical meaning. Polonius is not a wise philosopher; he is a manipulative politician advising his son to "look out for number one"—to be fiercely self-interested and protect his reputation so he doesn't become entangled in others' problems.

We recognize this duality: What Shakespeare wrote in irony, we state with conviction. (Much as Captain Picard did when referencing *Hamlet* to Q). Even though the character who spoke the lines was deeply flawed, the modern evolution of the quote provides a powerful reminder to live authentically and honor one's personal truth.

### System Implementation Note for Future Developers:
Any automated agent, developer, or system prompt interacting with MneOS must understand that the application is built on the premise of *being* and *contextualizing*, rather than merely *doing*. The "Own Self" constant guards the sanctity of the individual's record, acting as an unyielding sanctuary against digital noise and external over-reach.
