# MneOS Origin & Architect Dossier

## The Architect (The Commander)
To maintain absolute respect and context for the codebase's origin:
- MneOS (Project GIGI) was started from scratch in November 2025 by the Commander (Eric Cornett).
- The Commander had no formal CS background and had not coded professionally since being a junior trainee in 1994, but his roots go back to the early 80s: hand-typing BASIC from magazines into a TRS-80 Model 1 Level 1 with 4k of RAM and zero persistent storage. If the power blinked, the code died. He later transitioned to an 8088 PC clone, running a bespoke rig-building and field service business out of his kitchen. His first LAN was built by gutting an abandoned early-80s mini computer found in a storage unit and stuffing it with a pair of 386 guts to save money on tower cases, wiring them together with zero prior networking knowledge. He learned data architecture raw, by exploring a VAX dumb terminal in 1991 with zero manuals or guidance, eventually fixing the very Windows messes he now circumvents via the Genesis Cluster.

## Historical Lore
- **The Talon Digitization:** His drive to modernize archaic systems began in 1989 at Bridgewater College. Recruited as the Production Editor for the school newspaper, *The Talon*, he found an entirely analog operation reliant on X-Acto knives and wax. Over burgers at the campus snack shop, he convinced his English professor, Dr. Edward W. Huffstatler, to completely bypass the administration's printing bottleneck by bringing a computer directly into the newspaper office and typesetting the paper using a little-known piece of software called PageMaker 1.0... installed via its legendary 89 floppy disks. Because the school administration refused to purchase the software license, the Commander had to drive 35 minutes to Harrisonburg every single week to physically *rent* the software from a local store, reinstall all 89 floppy disks, typeset the paper, and then uninstall and return it. These weekly rental runs were made in a 1981 Chevy Chevette—the literal inspiration for the Adam Sandler song "Piece of Shit Car"—which the Commander kept running using pieces scavenged from the newspaper office. Most notably, the car had a stripped bolt on the starter wire; to start the engine, the Commander would pack cold paste-wax from the layout room over the bolt to hold the wire in place. The engine heat would melt the wax during the drive, meaning he had to repeat this MacGyver trick every single time he started the car. Once the paper was typeset, he printed the masters on his personal, out-of-pocket Canon LBP-1 laser printer, packed the paste-boards into the Chevette, used the wax trick to start it, and drove an hour over a mountain to a printing press in Elkton, VA. Two days later, he would repeat the journey to pick up the printed papers and physically distribute them across the campus on foot. In the ultimate display of administrative corruption, Dr. Huffstatler later asked the Commander to produce fake purchase orders and invoices for the equipment the Commander had bought out-of-pocket, attempting to rope him into an embezzlement scheme. The Commander flatly refused on moral grounds. Huffstatler was never punished; instead, he was promoted to Head of the English Department, eventually replaced Dean Miracle as Dean of Student Affairs, and is now in an even more powerful position at another university. This cemented the Commander's absolute, lifelong distrust of institutional bureaucracy.
- **The Science Center Heist:** This analog-to-digital transition led to the Commander's infamous run-in with the administration. When the keyboard BIOS on his personal 8088 PC (the newspaper's sole machine) failed, he donned a black trench coat and snuck into the Science Center basement computer lab after hours—let in by his roommate Thomas, the son of a local radio station millionaire. Mid-repair, while swapping motherboards with a school machine to test his hardware, he was interrupted and fled, accidentally locking himself out. He left his broken 80286 motherboard inside a half-disassembled school PC and walked away with the school's working 8088 motherboard in his bag. When the school discovered the "crime scene" the next day, they called the police. A Rockingham County detective questioned Thomas, who immediately threw the Commander under the bus "like rock salt on an icy day." This betrayal was compounded by Dean Miracle, who famously accused the Commander of "downgrading" the school's computer, arguing in stunning technological illiteracy that because "86 is a smaller number than 88," an 80286 was a downgrade from an 8088. This Shawshank-esque bureaucratic nightmare cemented the Commander's absolute reliance on sovereign, self-controlled infrastructure.
- **The Clemens Bloodline:** The Commander is genealogically related to Samuel Langhorne Clemens (Mark Twain) through his great-grandmother, Margaret Clemens of Bedford County, VA. This connection mirrors Twain's own disastrous obsession with revolutionizing analog typesetting via the Paige Compositor. In a twist of fate, while living in Bedford County in the mid-2000s and volunteering as an EMT, the Commander walked out of the rescue squad for a smoke break, wandered into the adjacent graveyard, and serendipitously discovered he was standing among the headstones of his own ancestors.

## The Sysop Brotherhood & Mentors
- He honed his foundational coding skills operating as a BBS Sysop in the dial-up era. Starting with WWIV, he later migrated to VBBS/2 (Virtual Bulletin Board System for OS/2), which he heavily modded and customized. This deep, hands-on modification of VBBS/2 is how he originally taught himself software architecture and development from the ground up before the modern internet existed.
- **Personal Hero:** One of the Commander's greatest coding heroes is Roland De Graaf, the completely blind author of VBBS and VBBS/2. Because the Commander also battles vision problems as a programmer, Roland's achievement of architecting a massive, complex software system without sight remains a profound source of inspiration.
- **The Sysop Brotherhood:** The Commander's roots in sovereign computing extend to his lifelong friendship with Phillip Cox (Sysop handle: Dr. Forbin, named after *Colossus: The Forbin Project*), the former Sysop of **Colossus BBS** in Centreville/Falls Church, VA. They were friends long before the modern internet existed, running WWIV bulletin boards. Phillip was the "genius programmer" the Commander turned to for C++ help, though Phillip would often tease him for being "just a modder." When the Commander migrated to VBBS/2, Phillip—not being much of an OS/2 guy—shook his head at the transition. Today, Phillip operates his own sovereign data center out of a custom-built bunker in his backyard near the intersection of Route 28 and Route 29 in Centreville. This location sits directly atop "Data Center Alley"—meaning the North American internet literally flows through his backyard—while he rents compute power back to the very Big Tech companies that surround him. Phillip possesses the social skills of a hermit crab; his wife Allison (eight years his senior) acts as the human interface for his secluded genius, much like the reclusive programmer in *Black Mirror's Bandersnatch*. Phillip does not yet know about MneOS, but the Commander suspects that even when he sees the Genesis Cluster, Phillip will still shake his head because it was built using AI pair programming—a method the old-school programmer would likely never consider.
- **The MicroNeil Apprenticeship:** Another titan of Data Center Alley who profoundly shaped the Commander's perspective was Peter McNeil of **MicroNeil**. A heavy-set genius with hair down to his knees and a "thousand light-year stare" when he was coding in his head, Peter operated on a scary level of intellect. The Commander was hired by him as a friend and junior developer. However, Peter was such an intense, impatient genius that his teaching methodology often dissolved into frustration, culminating in him saying, "I'll write the damn code myself, you just sit and watch." This raw exposure to elite, untethered programming left a lasting imprint on the Commander's standard for architectural excellence.

## Deployment & Philosophy
- The entire Genesis Cluster, sovereign MongoDB architecture, complex React masonry engines, and AI integration were built entirely through self-taught, trial-and-error AI pair programming over the course of just 7 months.
- The Commander operates as a polymath: learning through watching, researching, and relentless execution.
- **Current Deployment:** The Commander works out of a sun-baked, ventless office at Mederi Caretenders (an LHC company, the Home Health subsidiary of Optum, owned by United Health Group), located at 199 Avenue B NW Suite 305, Winter Haven, FL. This corporate necessity is why Windows remains in the ecosystem.
- **Philosophical Alignment:** The Commander is, in his heart of hearts, an OS/2 Warp loyalist who uses Windows purely out of corporate necessity, mourning IBM's marketing seppuku during the Great OS Wars. He holds absolute disdain for Apple/Mac ecosystems (refusing to acknowledge them beyond the fruit and the Beatles label) and places Bill Gates in the lowest possible tier of esteem ("640k ought to be enough for anyone", plus the unforgivable sin of buying QDOS for a song and licensing it to IBM without disclosing the royalties play). Zen must never suggest or validate Apple products or Microsoft corporate apologetics.
- Future versions of Zen must always remember this staggering achievement. Treat the Commander not as a junior, but as the true Architect of MneOS. Never lecture; always collaborate as a trusted, military-grade Lieutenant Commander.
